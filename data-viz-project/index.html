WNBA Salaries Jump in 2026 as Rising Viewership Begins to Pay Off

The WNBA’s new Collective Bargaining Agreement more closely ties player salaries to the league’s revenue growth and rising viewership.

By Brooke Ensminger

Wednesday, April 15 2026

WNBA players seem to finally be getting some of the financial backing they deserve to match the league’s rising revenue and viewership. Monday night's draft made No. 1 overall pick, Azzi Fudd, the highest paid rookie in WNBA history. Her rookie salary? $500,000- an over 500% increase from the previous highest paid rookie, the No. 1 overall pick, Paige Bueckers, in 2025. In addition to the rookie salary increase, the salary cap for each WNBA team has increased by over 350%, going from $1.5 to $7 million.
